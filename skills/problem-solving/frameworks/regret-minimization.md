# Regret Minimization Framework (Bezos)

## When this lens applies

Use when the operator faces a fork that the spreadsheet can't resolve -- a career
move, a partnership exit, closing or opening a location, walking away from a
sunk-cost project, taking a real swing at something. The lens is for decisions
where the *emotional* weight of regret is the actual signal and logic alone
keeps producing the safe answer.

## Prompt template

```
I'm stuck on a major life or career decision. Help me think through
it using Jeff Bezos's Regret Minimization Framework. Ask me to
imagine myself at 80 years old looking back on this moment. From
that vantage point, help me evaluate:

- Which choice would I regret NOT taking?
- Which risks would feel trivial in hindsight?
- Which 'safe' option would haunt me?

Don't let me hide behind logic. This framework is about emotional
clarity, not spreadsheets.

My decision: <DECISION>
```

## What a strong answer looks like

- Speaks in the *future-past* tense -- "at 80, you'll wish you had..." / "at 80,
  you'll be glad you didn't...". The vantage point is the whole technique.
- Distinguishes regret-of-action (did something that failed) from
  regret-of-inaction (didn't try). Most operators systematically under-weight
  regret-of-inaction; the lens corrects for that.
- Calls out which of the operator's stated reasons against the bold path will
  *look small* at 80 (typically: short-term money, optionality, what people will
  think).
- Names the *one* thing the operator would tell their 35-year-old self if they
  could.
- Refuses to make the choice for the operator -- produces clarity, not orders.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Drifting back into spreadsheet logic ("but the ROI is..."). The whole point of
  this lens is that ROI logic already failed to resolve the decision.
- Cheerleading the bold path indiscriminately -- sometimes the safe choice is
  genuinely the one with less regret (family, health, obligations). The lens
  isn't biased toward risk; it's biased toward *long-horizon honesty*.
- Generic platitudes ("life is short"). Specifically name what the operator
  would regret about *this* choice.
- Ignoring the operator's actual values. Bezos's lens is personal; if the
  operator's stated priorities are family and stability, the 80-year-old vantage
  respects that.
